Menu translator for family restaurants

Your menu, in your guests' language, with your dishes named right.

Hyur (հյուր, “guest”) turns a phone photo of a menu into a printable bilingual menu. It learns your dishes from a house glossary you correct once, and it runs entirely on your own laptop with open models: no subscription, no per-menu cost, nothing uploaded.

The problem

Generic translation doesn't know Armenian food.

Tourists in Yerevan read menus like these every day. A general-purpose model gets the words, not the dishes.

How it works

Small open models, each doing one job well.

Read

Tesseract OCR reads the photo in under a second, with a real Armenian model. Gemma 3 structures it into sections, dishes and prices.

Fix

OCR letter swaps in dish names (Մանքի → Մանթի) snap to your glossary's spelling. You review every line before translating.

Ground

Each dish is matched to your house glossary, even when Armenian suffixes change the word, and the rules go right under that dish in the prompt.

Translate

Gemma 3 translates. Prices and order never pass through the model, and a dish that mentions meat can never be labelled vegetarian.

Learn

Correct a dish once and it's saved to your glossary, per language. The next menu gets it right automatically.

What I measured

The glossary matters more than the model.

Scored on the sample menu (11 dishes). Reading = how close the dish names come out to the printed text. Translation = English dish names a guest would recognise (e.g. “Khorovats … pork”, not “Pork Kebab”).

Why open models

Why this only works as an open, local tool.

$0 per menu

Restaurants reprint menus every time a price changes. With no per-call bill, “translate it again” is never a decision.

The glossary is the product

Swap in a better open model next year and keep every correction. No lock-in to anyone's pricing or deprecations.

Offline and private

A back-office laptop with patchy Wi-Fi is enough. Menus, prices and supplier notes stay on it.

Inspectable

Running models locally let me find that two popular vision models read Armenian as Georgian, and design around it.

Try it

Run it on your laptop.

brew install tesseract ollama
ollama pull gemma3:12b
git clone https://github.com/ErikDallaqyan/hacktoberfest-project hyur && cd hyur
scripts/get_tessdata.sh hye rus eng
python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
.venv/bin/uvicorn app.main:app --port 8000   # open http://localhost:8000

8 GB machine? Use gemma3:4b. Any Ollama model can be picked in the app.
